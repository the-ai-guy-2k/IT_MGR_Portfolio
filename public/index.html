Executive biography

Path to IT Management

Sanil D Tison's path to IT management did not begin with a management title. It began in environments where technology, people, and mission were inseparable.

He entered the U.S. Navy in 2002 and spent roughly fourteen years in mission-critical technology work. The lesson was larger than the equipment: systems had owners, people had responsibilities, failures had consequences, and the mission still had to move. Leadership, accountability, planning, continuity, and technical judgment developed alongside engineering skill.

In civilian engineering, Tison carried that discipline into healthcare, defense research, distributed enterprise infrastructure, security, cloud, and automation. Those roles exposed him to the realities IT managers inherit every day — operational risk, technical debt, dependencies, change, outages, specialist knowledge, and competing priorities.

TAIG IT Solutions changed the level of responsibility. As Founder and Manager, Tison became accountable for more than the technical answer. He had to decide what mattered, understand client intent, shape services, coordinate people and providers, manage relationships, set priorities, and own outcomes.

That is the basis of his move into dedicated IT management. Engineering remains an advantage, not the destination. It gives Tison the technical credibility to understand the environment; management experience gives him the foundation to lead the people, resources, decisions, and operations around it.

The proposition is straightforward: put an experienced manager with deep engineering judgment in responsibility for an IT operation, and evaluate him by whether the organization becomes clearer, more reliable, better aligned, and better able to accomplish its mission.

Leadership philosophy

Advance the Truth Till Mission Completion

  1. Establish Intent

    Know what the organization is trying to accomplish and what success actually means.

  2. Establish Current Truth

    Understand the real state: people, systems, resources, risks, constraints, dependencies, evidence, and unresolved work.

  3. Advance the Truth

    Make decisions, assign work, execute, validate, update the picture, and continue until the intended outcome becomes reality.

Plans can change when reality changes. Responsibility does not. Tison's management approach is built around keeping decisions tied to evidence and keeping activity tied to mission.

People Leadership

Set clear ownership. Delegate at the right level. Give specialists room to exercise their discipline. Make priorities visible. Surface risk early. Develop people. Hold the team — and management — accountable for outcomes, not theater.

Decision Standard

  • What are we trying to accomplish?
  • What is true now?
  • What is blocking us?
  • Who owns the next action?
  • What evidence will tell us it worked?

Leadership framework

30 / 60 / 90-Day Plan

This is a method for entering an organization, not a prewritten diagnosis of one. The real plan begins after the environment is understood.

  1. Days 1–30

    Understand

    Meet the team and business stakeholders. Map critical services, systems, providers, projects, known risks, budget commitments, pain points, and unresolved work. Establish Current Truth before prescribing major change.

  2. Days 31–60

    Prioritize & Stabilize

    Validate the highest operational, security, people, lifecycle, and continuity risks. Clarify ownership and escalation. Stabilize material issues. Align the first improvement priorities with leadership.

  3. Days 61–90

    Execute & Improve

    Begin approved improvements with clear ownership and validation. Establish useful operating rhythms for people, services, risk, resources, and projects. Publish an updated Current Truth and a realistic forward plan.
